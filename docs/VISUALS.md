# Visual upgrade plan — "Against the Wall"

*Plan + record. §7 below tracks what has actually shipped (slice 1 landed in
`src/render/look.hpp`, `texture.hpp`, `shaders.hpp`). Style locked with the user:
**golden-hour ruin**, **procedural only — no asset files, ever**, full scope.*

---

## 1. Where the renderer is today

| What | Current state |
|---|---|
| Geometry | One instanced cube mesh, ≤81 instanced draws (one per resident chunk), 8 mosaic macro-cell patterns → varied brick *sizes* |
| Shading | `kCubeFS`: one `N·L` term, "AO" from `normal.y` only (top 1.05 / side 0.85 / bottom 0.55) |
| Colour | Per-brick `shade` byte lerped between two stone tones → monochrome, per-brick noise only |
| Sky | Analytic gradient + `pow(dot(dir,sun),500)` sun disc |
| Fog | `smoothstep(120,320,dist)` toward a flat `(0.66,0.71,0.79)` |
| Post | None (no tonemap, no bloom, no dither) |
| Player | Camera only: no hands, no body, no bob |
| Particles | None |
| Infra | **FBO + fullscreen blit already exist** (render-resolution scaling) → a post chain is nearly free to add |

### Three concrete things that already cost us "beauty" today

1. **The sun disagrees with itself.** Brick light is `normalize(0.45, 0.85, 0.35)`;
   the sky's sun is `normalize(0.5, 0.5, -0.45)`. Shadows and highlights can never
   line up with the bright spot in the sky, which is exactly the kind of thing
   that reads as "flat/unfinished" without the viewer knowing why.
2. **Fog is a constant grey, not air.** It has no relation to the sky in the view
   direction, so distance looks dirty instead of atmospheric.
3. **The world is one brick deep.** It is a *sheer wall*, so lighting alone will
   always look like wallpaper: all relief comes from bricks that are pulled out.
   Beauty has to come from the wall *pattern*, the pulled-brick shadows, the sky,
   the player's presence and post-processing — not from "more polygons".

---

## 2. Guardrails (non-negotiable, they are the project's identity)

- **Zero asset files.** The `.exe` is self-contained and CI needs no asset
  pipeline. Anything we add must be *generated in code* (textures at startup) or
  embedded as generated byte arrays.
- **Determinism.** Visual work must not touch `wall/player/interaction` semantics;
  the headless demo (`fps=60, chunks=81, inst=7255`) must stay bit-identical.
- **60 fps on integrated GPUs**, ≤ ~2 ms combined for post + shadows at 1080p.
- **Every shader degrades gracefully**: if a fancy shader fails to compile, fall
  back to today's flat one rather than showing nothing.
- **Test what can be tested**: CPU-side generators (patterns, palettes, AO) get
  unit tests; shaders get screenshot/bench tooling instead.

## 3. Pick a style first (this is the real decision)

### A. Golden-hour ruin *(recommended)*
Warm low sun on pale stone, long shadows, dust motes in the light, deep blue
haze in the distance, sky that goes from hot orange at the horizon to cold blue
overhead. Altitude drives a slow time-of-day palette shift.

- Why it wins: it is the look that forgives the least detail. A low sun + haze +
  bloom makes *simple* geometry look photographic, because the eye reads the
  atmosphere, not the polygons.
- Cost: low. All procedural (palette + sky + fog + a soft sun + post).
- Risk: none technical; needs taste in the palette.

### B. Blueprint at dusk
Near-black wall, emissive cyan/magenta edges, hairline construction grid, rim
light, fog as flat colour bands. Reads as *deliberate* rather than realistic.

- Why: cheapest option of all (edge glow + palette + grid overlay), very
  "designed", hides low geometry detail behind strong graphic shapes.
- Cost: lowest. Risk: can feel cold/tech-demo-ish if overdone.

### C. Brutalist concrete, hard noon
Grey concrete, one hard shadow direction, high contrast, minimal colour, heavy
grain. Needs real shadowing + AO to work at all — it is the most technically
demanding and the most bland if we cut corners.

**Hybrid worth considering:** A at low altitude → C in the middle band → B at the
top (you climb out of the haze into cold thin air where the wall reads as
blueprint/scan lines). That turns "climbing" into a visual story with three
clearly different bands, and it is a *procedural* arc, so still zero assets.

## 4. The no-asset toolkit (ranked by payoff ÷ effort)

### P0 — cheap, high impact (do first)

1. **One source of truth for the look** — new `src/render/look.hpp`: sun
   direction/elevation/colour, zenith/horizon/ground colours, fog density,
   exposure, bloom threshold, plus palette ramps. Sky, bricks, fog, UI accents
   and *shadows* then agree by construction (fixes §1.1).
2. **Sky-driven fog** — compute the fog colour from the same sky function
   (`fogColor(dir)`), so the wall dissolves into the sky in the view direction.
   Photographic, ~20 lines, fixes §1.2.
3. **Richer brick response** — replace the fake `normal.y` AO with:
   hemisphere ambient (sky above, ground-bounce below), a warm key + cool fill
   split, a fresnel rim to separate silhouettes, and a roughness-driven specular
   so sunlit faces get a soft sheen.
4. **Post chain in the existing blit** — bloom (bright-pass + 2 blur passes at
   half res), ACES filmic tonemap, ordered dither (kills the 8-bit banding in the
   sky gradient), vignette, subtle edge chromatic aberration, light grain,
   optional FXAA. We already have the FBO + fullscreen pass, so this is mostly
   new fragment code: ~150 lines for a completely different perceived quality.
5. **Large-scale colour variation** — extend the per-brick `shade` byte with a
   second, low-frequency "weathering" byte sampled at chunk build (damp streaks,
   soot, moss bands, sun-bleached patches). Per-brick noise says "noisy";
   low-frequency structure says "designed".

### P1 — signature features

6. **Self-shadowing by grid raymarch** *(the clever one)* — the wall is a grid,
   so instead of a shadow map we upload a small window of brick occupancy as an
   `R8` 3D texture (needs 4 new entries in `gl.h`: `TexImage3D` etc., all GL 3.3
   core) and raymarch ~24 steps along the sun direction in the fragment shader.
   Protruding bricks then cast real shadows onto the flat wall and their
   neighbours. Ties directly into data we already have, no light matrices.
   Fallback: classic 2048² depth shadow map (more code, same result).
7. **CPU contact AO** — when a chunk's instances are built we know which
   neighbours are pulled out, so compute a per-brick/edge AO factor on the CPU
   and pack it into the instance data. Free at render time, and it is the cue
   that makes pulled bricks feel *attached* instead of floating.
8. **Procedural stone textures (no files)** — bake at startup: 512² fBm noise →
   albedo (with mortar darkening and pitting) + height → Sobel normal map, into
   real mipmapped GL textures. Halves the distance-aliasing sparkle that flat
   colours produce today. UVs come from world position (triplanar by face axis),
   so the mesh format does not change.

### P2 — the player and the world ("juice")

9. **Presence**: procedural hands (two rounded boxes/capsules in view space, same
   material, idle sway + a reach animation while a brick lerps), camera bob,
   landing dip and a small FOV kick on jumps. Enormous perceived-quality win for
   a few hundred lines and zero assets.
10. **Debris & dust**: pulling a brick spawns instanced cubes that fall, tumble,
    shrink and fade (the instancing path already exists); landings puff a couple
    of additive billboards; motes drift in the sun shafts.
11. **Interaction language**: outline the targeted brick with animated dashes,
    draw a progress arc for the 3 s pull/push, and a small ghost preview of the
    final position. Makes the core mechanic legible *and* looks intentional.
12. **Altitude arc**: haze thins with height, sky darkens toward space,
    procedural stars fade in, wind streaks accelerate, palette shifts as in §3.
13. **Parallax depth**: procedurally generated distant mosaic silhouettes
    (instanced quads at 2–3 depth layers) so the horizon is not empty.
14. **HUD in-style**: same palette, blueprint tick marks, animated row highlight,
    subtle grain over the menu panel.

### P3 — generator-level beauty (testable!)

15. **Break the wallpaper**: the wall generator decides — deterministically, per
    macro-cell — between solid wall, a *hole* (dark recess revealing an interior
    void, with a lit rim), a protruding ledge/module, a vent/pipe pair, or a
    damaged field. This changes nothing about climbing mechanics but changes the
    silhouette of the entire climb, and it lives in `grid.hpp`/`chunk.hpp`, which
    we *can* unit-test (pattern coverage, no gaps/collisions, stable across
    chunk reloads).

### P4 — if we ever allow real assets

| Asset | Payoff | Cost to the project |
|---|---|---|
| HDRI sky for image-based lighting | Glassy, believable ambient | One file (or a decoded-in-code gradient), breaks "zero files" unless embedded |
| glTF hand/tool model | Real hands instead of boxes | A loader (`cgltf` is one header, still third-party) or a hand-rolled subset; asset pipeline + CI changes |
| Texture atlas (stone/metal decals) | Variety beyond procedural noise | Files, licensing, exe size, reproducibility |

Recommendation: stay procedural for now. If we want a signature asset later, do
it *once* (hands or an HDRI), embed it as a generated byte array, and keep the
"double-click one `.exe`" story intact.

## 5. Tooling to iterate on looks without guessing

Shipped:

- `F3` in-game overlay: frame time, fps, chunks, drawn instances, altitude, sun
  elevation, fog density, star amount and whether the look pipeline and post pass
  are active (so a frame cost can be attributed to each feature).
- `F4`: toggle the whole post pass live — the fastest A/B between the graded
  image and the raw scene.
- `--bench [--novsync] [--frames N]`: runs the *scripted* camera path (the same
  one headless mode uses) and prints avg / p50 / p95 / worst frame times. The
  scripted path is what makes two runs comparable — across settings, machines or
  builds. `--novsync` asks the driver to drop the swap interval so the numbers
  are not simply the refresh rate.
- `--no-post`, `--hud`: start with post off / the overlay on.
- Graceful shader fallback (see §2): if any look shader fails to compile, the
  legacy flat shading is used and the GLSL error is logged, so the game never
  blanks.

Still to come: `--shots <dir>` (fixed poses → BMP files, own writer, no deps) for
before/after contact sheets.

## 7. What landed (slice 1) — and where the knobs are

**One source of truth for light.** `src/render/look.hpp` owns the sun, the sky
palette, the fog and the post parameters. The CPU picks the palette for the
player's current altitude (`lookAtAltitude`), the shaders get the same values as
uniforms plus a shared GLSL block (`lookGLSL()`), and `brickProfileGLSL()`
*generates* the stone-profile function from the same C++ table the texture baker
uses, so the shader and the textures cannot drift apart. The old mismatch — brick
light `(0.45, 0.85, 0.35)` vs sky sun `(0.5, 0.5, -0.45)` — is gone by
construction.

**Altitude palette (keyframes at 0 / 100 / 320 units).** Ground level is heavy
warm haze with the sun just above the horizon; mid-climb is clear golden hour
with long shadows; high up the air thins, the sky goes deep blue, the sun climbs
and stars come out. Everything interpolates: sun direction *and* colour, zenith /
horizon / ground colours, haze, cloud cover, star amount, fog density, exposure,
bloom, vignette. Tuning lives in `lookKeys()` — one table.

**Sky.** Full-screen ray pass with: horizon→zenith gradient, warm haze band,
below-horizon ground fade, sun disc + wide glow, drifting two-octave cloud layer
projected on a plane above the camera, and twinkling stars where the air is thin.
`awSkyAir()` is the cheap subset (gradient + haze + glow) used for aerial
perspective on geometry, so distance fades into *the sky that is actually behind
it* instead of a flat grey.

**Bricks.** Procedural stone: a 2D texture *array* (8 stone types × 256²) baked in
code at startup, each layer carrying four quadrant sub-tiles — mortar, clean
face, pitted, water-stained — blended per fragment by a per-brick hash (crack
scatter, edge occlusion, downward staining). The alpha channel stores *height*,
which is used two ways: baked occlusion *and* derivative bump mapping
(`dFdx/dFdy` of the height, no extra texture fetches). Side faces — the ones
looking into the mortar gap — get the mortar tile outright. Texture density is
constant in world units, so a 4 m slab tiles stone instead of stretching it.
Lighting is hemisphere ambient (coloured by the actual sky palette) + warm key
with a soft hazy terminator + cool fill + rim + broad stone sheen, per-type
roughness, then distance flattening and aerial perspective.

**Mortar behind the bricks.** A per-chunk textured plane just behind the deepest
brick body. The hairline gaps between jittered bricks read as mortar (they used
to let the sky through), and a pulled brick reveals a real crevasse: the plane is
lit as an ambient-only gap with a little sun spill.

**Post.** Half-resolution bright pass → separable gaussian → ACES tonemap +
exposure + vignette + static dither (kills banding in the big sky gradient) and a
whisper of grain, applied in the pass that also upscales the scene to the window.
The scene now always renders through the offscreen target when post is on.

**Debris and dust.** `src/game/debris.hpp` is a fixed 640-particle pool of stone
chips and dust motes — spawned on every started pull/push (dust at the brick
face) and on landing (a ring scaled by impact speed), plus a sparse ambient
drift of motes so the low sun has something to catch. They are drawn as tiny
cubes by the *brick* program, so a chip catches the same sun, sits in the same
fog and wears the same stone (its profile comes from the strata of the wall
around it) at the cost of one extra instanced draw call and no new shader. They
shrink out as they expire instead of needing alpha, and the pool is a hard cap,
so frame time cannot spike.

**Strata.** Each ~96-brick band of altitude uses a different mix of the eight
stone profiles (`familyWeightsFor`), with a slow horizontal drift so the band
boundaries are not flat lines. The bottom of the wall starts in the baseline
course; climb and the rock changes — granite bands, terracotta bands, crumbled
bands near the top. Pure function of (bx, by): a chunk always regenerates
identically, and old saves do not shift.

**Determinism.** None of this touches simulation: the headless baseline is
unchanged (`fps=60 chunks=81 inst=7255 mods=45 peakY=44`), and the palette and
textures are pure functions covered by unit tests (`testLookPalette`,
`testLookSky`, `testStoneTextures` — 538 new assertions).

**Perf notes.** New per-frame cost: one extra half-res chain (3 small passes), one
textured plane per visible chunk, and a heavier brick fragment shader (4 texture
fetches + derivative bump). Everything is behind one flag: `F4` toggles post,
`--no-post` starts with it off; if the look shaders are rejected the legacy path
runs unchanged.
